A real learning process

Assess. Learn. Apply. Improve.

Every student is different. After approval and login, your assessment shapes a pathway for your industry, role, confidence and business objectives.

STEP 01

Get assessed

Answer eight questions covering AI literacy, prompting, verification, data, workflow design, measurement, governance and delivery.

STEP 02

Pick your pathway

Choose from 12 industries and 13 business functions, including CEO, CFO, COO, CIO, HR, sales and operations.

STEP 03

Do the work

Follow clear instructions using approved or synthetic data. Build a useful deliverable and record what you learned.

STEP 04

Track progress

Move to more advanced tasks after documenting outcomes, checks and human review. Completion is not an external certification.

Frequently asked questions

Know exactly what you're signing up for.

Is Nand AI Academy really free?

Yes. The published Academy courses and exercises are currently free. Registration is required and access is reviewed manually.

Do I need technical or programming experience?

No. Start at Basic or Beginner. You can choose your own entry level and develop more advanced technical skills if relevant to your goals.

Is access automatic after registration?

No. Complete the registration request. Once reviewed and approved, you will receive login instructions by email. Student accounts are invitation-based.

Are the courses accredited by MIT, Stanford or Oracle?

No. Nand AI Academy is independent. We link to official external learning materials but do not claim affiliation, accreditation or endorsement.

Will learning AI protect my job?

No training programme can guarantee employment. Our goal is to help people gain practical capabilities, make informed decisions and adapt as work changes.

AI should empower humanity.
Not make it obsolete.

Practical AI knowledge should be accessible to everyone—not reserved for those who can afford expensive training. Education is one part of a responsible response to AI displacement, inequality and safety risks. Human oversight, governance and accountability matter too.
